## Slide 1 — The problem（0:45）
**画面：** 室外 / 有空调的室内两个极端；《Hot Tea, Rain City》的引语或照片。
**提示：** 两份市政文件都是定性的，不要说成数据。

> Vancouver has a lot of public space, but in winter it has much less public life.
> Part of the reason is that our buildings offer only two conditions: outside, or a fully conditioned interior. There is very little in between.
> The City has heard this from the public. In the 2018 *Hot Tea, Rain City* engagement, people asked for covered seating, heated covered areas and warm lighting, so they could stay outside longer. The 2021 *Downtown Public Space Strategy* calls for "year-round" and "rain-friendly" public spaces.
> So the question I am working on is: how much conditioning does public life actually need?

---

## Slide 2 — Statement（0:30）
**画面：** 只放一句话。
**提示：** 用哪一句、作 topic 还是 thesis，你还没定。下面是两个候选。

> [候选 A，10-15 原句] My statement is: *Conditioned space can be reinterpreted as a calibrated gradient between climatic protection and environmental exposure, supporting year-round public occupation in Vancouver.*
>
> [候选 B，语气更强] My statement is: *Rather than a binary of conditioned and unconditioned, conditioned space should be calibrated as a gradient between climatic protection and environmental exposure, giving each activity just enough protection to sustain year-round public occupation in Vancouver.*

> In short: not *whether* a space is conditioned, but *how much*.

---

## Slide 3 — The claim and the argument（1:00）
**画面：** 论证链总图（问题 → 主张 → 前提 → 规则 → 检验 → 缺口）+ 五段剖面色带。
**提示：** 这张图是全场导航，后面每页开头指一下它。

> My research is a logical argument, and this diagram is its structure. I will walk through it from left to right.
> The claim is that conditioning is a gradient, not a switch. I define five stages, by how a space is enclosed.
> **Exposed** has no roof. **Protected** has a roof. **Buffered** has a roof and at least two walls. **Tempered** is fully enclosed but not mechanically conditioned. **Controlled** is mechanically conditioned.
> The argument has a direction. The building's massing decides how much protection a space has. The protection decides which activities it can host, in each season.
> And the principle is "just enough": each activity should get the lowest stage that still works for it. Anything higher is over-conditioning.

---

## Slide 4 — Premises from the literature（2:00）
**画面：** 三列（P1 / P2 / P3），每列三行：文献 / 它说了什么 / → 推出的规则。最后小块放先例和定位（或 A2 第 7 页的二维图）。
**提示：** Rahm、Lacaton & Vassal 只看过网页，只说"是先例"，不说数字。

> The argument rests on three premises from the literature.
>
> **Premise one: the binary is not necessary.** Mora and Bean explain that the adaptive comfort model lets indoor temperatures float, but only when the outdoor mean is above 10 degrees. In Vancouver, that fails from about November to April. Kim finds people tolerate more variation under natural ventilation than under air conditioning. And Hou's review of semi-outdoor spaces finds a wider tolerance, but only when people can come and go freely.
> So: seated activities need a controlled space in winter, and spaces people pass through can be buffered.
>
> **Premise two: comfort depends on the activity and the person.** Zhang finds that the more intense the activity, the cooler people prefer it. Lau, studying winter parks in Hong Kong, finds that people over 75 prefer conditions about four degrees warmer. There is no comfort study for Vancouver, so I borrow ranges from Cambridge and Sheffield, whose seasonal temperatures are almost the same as ours.
> So, vigorous activity can stay tempered, and spaces for older adults and children go one stage up.
>
> **Premise three: comfort shapes use, but does not decide it.** In Montreal, Zacharias found the share of people sitting tracks air temperature very closely, but presence is not satisfaction. In Gothenburg, weather explains about half of the variation in attendance.
> So my rules only say whether a space *can* host an activity. They do not claim to create social life.
>
> Conceptually this builds on Philippe Rahm's meteorological architecture and Lacaton and Vassal's winter gardens. The gap: most semi-outdoor comfort studies are in hot, humid climates. Very few address a mild, wet winter like ours, or take rain into account.

## Slide 6a — From rules to stages（0:40）
**画面：** R1–R8 表，每条用颜色标依据类型：推断 / 借用 / 假设 / 我的决定。
**提示：** 8 条里 5 条来自文献推断（R1–R4、R6），R5 是假设，R7、R8 是你的决定。

> To test the argument, I wrote it as eight rules. For example: an activity that must stay dry needs at least a protected space; a seated activity needs a controlled space in winter.
> Each rule is labelled by where it comes from. Five are inferred from the literature. One is an assumption, and two are my own design decisions. I want this to be visible, because a logical argument is only as strong as its premises.

## Slide 6b — Running the argument on a building（0:50）
**画面：** 录屏前半段（体块 → 阶段 → 功能排开）；A/B/C/D 四个方案的小图 + 对比表。
**提示：** 数字只讲成"方案之间的相对值"。

> I then ran the rules on a real program, the Marpole community centre brief, on a site in Sunset Park. The community centre is not my subject. It is the test bed: a public interior that is open all year, with a clear list of activities.
> Each activity takes its lowest winter stage, and the plan arranges itself from buffered at the edge, through tempered, to controlled, which gathers on the winter-sun side.
> With the same program and four different massing options, the gradient and the unassigned space change. These numbers are only for comparing options with each other, not absolute performance.

## Slide 6c — Every decision can be traced（0:50）
**画面：** 录屏后半段：点一个房间，显示贴哪面墙、和谁相邻、属于哪个阶段、阶段从哪条规则来。

> The most important part for me is this. You can click any room and ask: why is it here?
> The tool shows which façade it uses, what it is next to, and which stage it needs, traced back to the rule and to where that rule came from.
> So the argument is not hidden inside the tool. Any decision can be questioned, and if a premise is wrong, you can see exactly which rooms it affects.

## Slide 7 — What it shows, what it doesn't, next steps（0:40）
**画面：** 两栏：Can show / Cannot show yet；下面三条下一步。

> So far, the test shows that the argument can be written as rules, that it produces a recognisable layered organisation, and that every decision can be traced.
> It does not yet show three things: how much mechanically conditioned area the gradient saves compared with a conventional all-controlled layout; whether my five stages really match different levels of thermal protection; and whether a tempered space is comfortable in a Vancouver winter.
> These are my next steps, starting with that comparison against an all-controlled baseline. The current version also still weighs compactness and sun before the gradient, which I will change.
