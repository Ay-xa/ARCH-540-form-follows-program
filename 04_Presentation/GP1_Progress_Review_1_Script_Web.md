## 中文版

### 0. 开场（0:20）
【画面】首页。标题 Just Enough，设问一句，桥下照片。开讲时把推拉条从左拉到右，雨天图盖上来。

各位好。我的题目是 Just Enough，调节作为一种校准过的渐变。先看这张照片：温哥华一座桥下的野餐桌，干天有人坐；把条拉过去，是同一个地方的冬天，雨从侧面打进来，没人坐得住。研究就从这里开始。

### 1. 问题（1:00）
【画面】滚到 Problem。右边一道墙升起："外面是天气，里面是一个设定"。文献句子一句句划红。

温哥华的公共生活被天气管着，冬天的雨和冷让一些公共活动很难持续。而建筑通常只用一道边界来组织环境控制：外面是天气，里面是一个设定。

我的第一个观察：不同的活动需要的环境不一样。跑步、喝咖啡、在电脑前坐几个小时，要的温度、风、遮雨和稳定性都不同。

问题是：为什么早期排布很少用这种适应能力来决定一个空间需要多少防护？我们通常先定房间，再去想条件。

文献里有四条线索。市政府的文件说，公众要的是有遮蔽、有暖、不怕雨的地方。乡土建筑曾经是调和气候，不是把气候挡在外面。Mora 和 Bean 指出 ASHRAE 55 本身就是二分的：PMV 主动调节模型，或者被动适应性模型，中间什么都没有；而被动适应性模型要求的 10 度，温哥华十一月到四月都不够。最后，半室外空间的研究多在湿热气候，温和多雨的冬天几乎没人研究；现有工具也没有一个把"活动需要多少防护"放进排布。两个空白。

### 2. 命题（0:35）
【画面】滚到 Thesis。墙裂成五段色带。

所以我问：如果调节不是房间的固定属性，而是气候、活动、建筑和季节之间一种会变的关系呢？

命题：受调节的空间可以被重新理解为一个校准过的渐变，从气候防护到暴露；活动各自的环境需求和微气候条件，共同引导空间排布和防护策略。

研究问题：活动各自的环境要求，如何在早期的空间组织里起作用，既减少过度调节，又保住可接受的舒适和可用性。

### 3. 方法，以及我在哪一步（0:30）
【画面】滚到 Method。路线图完整画出，段末出现红色虚线框 CURRENT STAGE。

主干是逻辑论证。GP1 从观察、批判、研究到转化；GP2 从实验到建筑综合，再到开放的结尾，每一步旁边是用到的方法。红框是我现在的位置：批判和研究之间。我制作的工具能跑，测试活动和空间的关系已经开始；空间策略和 kit-of-parts 还没整理出来。

### 4. 批判：不是开或关，而是五段（0:50）
【画面】滚到 Critique。路线图收到 Critique；然后五张剖面从左到右逐段加围合。

Critique 的部分是我对单一调节模式的挑战。我认为机会在于：不同活动需要不同条件，而空间组织还没用上这个差异。建筑常把舒适当作排布完成之后再提供的东西。

我的主张：调节不是开或关，而是从暴露到受控的五段渐变，每个活动只拿刚好够的防护。活动和气候的关系构成渐变，渐变再变成空间组织的原则。

五段按围合定义：没屋顶是 exposed；有屋顶、少于两面墙是 protected；有屋顶、至少两面墙是 buffered；四面封闭、没有机械调节是 tempered；有机械调节是 controlled。

### 5. 方向（0:40）
【画面】滚到 Direction。三个圆合拢成韦恩图，中间是 vernacular intelligence。

我整理出了三个基石来细化研究：Climate Infrastructure、Spatial Behaviour 和 Inhabitable Climate Threshold。其中 Climate Infrastructure 的体块决定防护程度，防护程度决定空间能承接哪些活动，也就是 Spatial Behaviour；空间策略，也就是 Inhabitable Climate Threshold，再进一步调整条件。三个圆重叠的地方，就是我想找到的、运用现代技术重新呈现的乡土智慧。

怎么检验：现在用规则和模拟比较，然后配置空间；下一步加 AI 辅助解释空间配置需求，再用基线和实测检验。结果要的不是一个最优解，而是一套逻辑，把环境性能更早带进设计决策，并且可比较、可核对。最终从性能评价，走向以性能为依据的空间设计；从固定设定的固定房间，走向能随气候和活动改变的建筑。

### 6. 前提：文献（1:30）
【画面】滚到 Premises。路线图高光 Research；韦恩图分开成三个圆，各带一个子问题。再滚到 Activity，圆放大，图表逐项出现，红句子划出。

研究这一步探索了三个问题。空间：哪些策略能不靠机械系统提供这些条件？活动：不同活动需要什么条件，能容忍多大变化？决策：建筑师怎么在早期比较它们、翻译成排布？

先讲活动，三个前提。

第一，二分法不是必需的。Kim：自然通风下人能容忍的温度变化比空调下更大。Hou：人能自由来去时，容忍范围宽到预测的两到三倍。Pérez-Fargallo：人觉得冷热先调整自己，再去开暖气。中间几段可以先靠人适应。

第二，舒适取决于活动和人。Zhang 和 Lau：活动越剧烈，中性体感温度就越低；75 岁以上的中性体感温度高大约 4 度。中性体感温度阈值是从剑桥和谢菲尔德借的，温哥华没有本地研究。Nikolopoulou：同样的天气秋天和春天感觉不同，自愿来的人比路过的更能忍。Krüger 在格拉斯哥测到的中性区间，比原始范围低，指出寒冷环境下的人可以接受相对更低的温度。

第三，舒适影响使用，但不决定使用。蒙特利尔坐下的人数跟气温走，但在场不等于满意。

### 7. 空间：策略积累（0:35）
【画面】滚到 Space。圆里八个线稿，然后三张手稿从左到右揭开。

空间这一步还在收集策略，做成 kit-of-parts。乡土的：廊和拱廊、院子、热质量、可开合的围护。Hou 给寒冷地区的：优先冬季阳光，封住迎风面，半透明屋顶。被动的：冬季花园作缓冲，日照和辐射最关键。手稿是三个零件：VanDusen 的 oculus，日本的 gradient 空间，和 Bahrain 的 cooling station。

### 8. 决策：规则 R1 到 R8（0:50）
【画面】滚到 Decision。路线图高光 Translation；点规则，色带上出现冬夏两个标记。

转译这部分驱动决策这一步，把文献变成八条规则。对每个活动、每个季节，找出它触发的所有规则，取要求最高的那一段作为最低阶段，这就是"刚好够"：低于它接不住活动，高于它就是过度调节。第一版现在只覆盖冬季。

举几条。R1，必须干燥的活动至少 protected，依据温哥华的遮雨导则。R3，久坐的活动冬季至少 controlled，依据 Mora 和 Bean 的 10 度门槛。R4，剧烈活动 tempered 就够。八条里五条来自文献，一条假设，两条是我的决定，表里都标了。

### 9. 用工具检验（0:50）
【画面】滚到 Test。结果柱状图和工具截图。

工具是论证能运行的版本，不是成果本身。它给出空间的防护等级，生成和比较排布，并且解释：点一个房间就能看到它需要哪种防护、基于什么规则和证据。

它证明了论证可以写成规则并运行，能产生分层，每个决定可追溯。还没证明的：渐变比全部 controlled 省多少调节面积，对照组和计算优化是下一步；五段是否对应真实的热防护；tempered 在温哥华冬天是否舒适。

### 10. 下一步与两个问题（0:20）
【画面】滚到 What comes next。路线图仍在 Research；韦恩图回来，上方是结束语。

下一步还在研究这一步：活动和空间的联系是论证里最薄的一环，要读更多案例，把 kit-of-parts 做出来，让每个策略都能放到渐变上、在工具里测。

两个问题请教两位：没有本地实测，这个渐变有说服力吗？从活动到空间到决策这条逻辑，哪里需要理顺？谢谢。

## English version

### 0. Opening (0:20)
[Screen] Hero. Title, the question, the bridge photo. While speaking, drag the bar from left to right so the winter image covers the dry one.

Good afternoon. My title is Just Enough: conditioning as a calibrated gradient. This is a picnic table under a bridge in Vancouver. In dry weather people sit here. Pull the bar across: the same place in winter, rain from the side, nobody stays. The project starts here.

### 1. Problem (1:00)
[Screen] Scroll to Problem. A wall rises on the stage: outside, the weather; inside, one setting. The literature sentences highlight one by one.

Public life in Vancouver is shaped by weather; winter rain and cold make some public activities hard to sustain. Yet buildings organise environmental control around one boundary: weather outside, one setting inside.

My first observation: different activities need different environments. Running, having coffee and working at a laptop for hours do not need the same temperature, wind, shelter or stability. So why does early-stage layout so rarely use that to decide how much protection a space needs? We fix the rooms first, then the conditions.

Four threads in the literature point here. City documents show the public asking for sheltered, heated, rain-friendly places to stay. Vernacular buildings once mediated climate rather than shutting it out. Mora and Bean show that ASHRAE 55 is itself a binary: PMV, the model for actively conditioned buildings, or the adaptive model for passively conditioned ones, with nothing between; and the adaptive model's ten-degree threshold fails here from November to April. And semi-outdoor research sits in hot, humid climates, while no tool puts an activity's need for protection into the layout. Two gaps.

### 2. Thesis (0:35)
[Screen] Scroll to Thesis. The wall splits into the five bands.

So I ask: what if conditioning is not a fixed property of a room, but a changing relationship between climate, activity, architecture and season?

The thesis: conditioned space can be reinterpreted as a calibrated gradient of climatic protection and exposure, where activity-specific environmental needs and microclimatic conditions guide spatial arrangement and protection strategies.

The research question: how can activity-specific requirements inform early-stage spatial organisation, reducing over-conditioning while keeping acceptable comfort and usability?

### 3. Method, and where I am (0:30)
[Screen] Scroll to Method. The roadmap draws itself; at the end the red dashed box marks the current stage.

Logical argumentation is the backbone. GP1 moves from observation to critique, research and translation; GP2 from experiment to synthesis and an open ending, each step with its own tactics. The red box is where I am: between critique and research. The tool I built runs, and testing the relationship between activity and space has started; the spatial strategies and the kit-of-parts are not yet compiled.

### 4. Critique: not on or off, but five stages (0:50)
[Screen] Scroll to Critique. The roadmap narrows to Critique, then the five sections assemble from left to right.

The critique is my challenge to the single mode of conditioning. I see the opportunity here: different activities need different conditions, and spatial organisation has not used that difference. Architecture treats comfort as something provided after the plan is organised.

My claim: conditioning is not on or off but a five-stage gradient from exposed to controlled, and each activity gets just enough protection. The relationship between activity and climate builds the gradient; the gradient becomes principles for spatial organisation.

The stages are defined by enclosure. No roof: exposed. A roof with fewer than two walls: protected. A roof with at least two walls: buffered. Fully enclosed without mechanical conditioning: tempered. Mechanically conditioned: controlled.

### 5. Direction (0:40)
[Screen] Scroll to Direction. The three fields close into the Venn diagram, vernacular intelligence at the centre.

I have set out three cornerstones to sharpen the research: climate infrastructure, spatial behaviour and the inhabitable climate threshold. The massing of the climate infrastructure determines protection; protection determines which activities a space can host, that is the spatial behaviour; and spatial strategies, the inhabitable climate threshold, adjust the conditions further. Where the three circles overlap is the vernacular intelligence I want to find again and present with today's technology.

How it is tested: for now, rules and simulation compare options and then configure the space; next, AI-assisted explanation of what each space needs, then a baseline and measurements. The outcome is not one optimal answer but a logic that brings environmental performance into design decisions earlier, comparable and checkable. In the end, from performance evaluation to performance-informed spatial design; from fixed rooms with fixed settings towards architecture that changes with climate and activity.

### 6. Premises from the literature (1:30)
[Screen] Scroll to Premises: the roadmap lights Research, then the Venn opens into three fields with their questions. Scroll into Activity: the circle grows, the chart builds, the red sentences sweep in.

The research step explores three questions. Space: which strategies provide those conditions without mechanical systems? Activity: what conditions do activities need, and how much variation can they tolerate? Decision: how can architects compare them early and translate them into layout?

Activity first, three premises.

One: the binary is not necessary. Kim: under natural ventilation people tolerate more variation than under air conditioning. Hou: when people can come and go freely, the tolerated range is two to three times wider than predicted. Pérez-Fargallo: people adjust themselves before they turn on heating. The intermediate stages can rely on people adapting first.

Two: comfort depends on the activity and the person. Zhang and Lau: the more intense the activity, the lower the neutral felt temperature; over seventy-five it is about four degrees higher. The neutral-temperature thresholds are borrowed from Cambridge and Sheffield, because Vancouver has no local study. Nikolopoulou: the same weather feels different in autumn and spring, and people who come by choice tolerate more. Krüger's neutral band in Glasgow sits below the original range, which suggests that people in cold places accept relatively lower temperatures.

Three: comfort shapes use but does not decide it. In Montreal sitting tracks air temperature, but presence is not satisfaction.

### 7. Space: collecting strategies (0:35)
[Screen] Scroll to Space. Eight glyphs in the circle, then the three sketches wipe in.

The space step is still collecting strategies into a kit-of-parts. Vernacular: veranda and arcade, courtyard, thermal mass, operable envelope. Hou, for colder climates: winter sun first, enclose the windward side, a translucent roof. Passive: the winter garden as a buffer, with sun and radiation as the key levers. The sketches are three parts: the VanDusen oculus, a Japanese gradient of spaces, and a cooling station in Bahrain.

### 8. Decision: rules R1 to R8 (0:50)
[Screen] Scroll to Decision. The roadmap lights Translation; select a rule and the winter and summer markers land on the band.

The translation part drives the decision step: it turns the literature into eight rules. For each activity in each season, every rule it triggers is found, and the highest stage asked for becomes its minimum. That minimum is just enough: below it the space cannot host the activity, above it the space is over-conditioned. The first version, for now, covers winter only.

A few of them. R1: activities that must stay dry, at least protected, from the Vancouver rain-shelter guideline. R3: seated activities, controlled in winter, from the ten-degree threshold in Mora and Bean. R4: vigorous activity, tempered is enough. Five of eight come from the literature, one is an assumption, two are my decisions; the table says which.

### 9. Testing with the tool (0:50)
[Screen] Scroll to Test. The result bars and two screenshots of the tool.

The tool is the runnable version of the argument, not the result. It assigns each space a level of protection, generates and compares layouts, and explains: click a room and you see which protection it needs, and the rule and evidence behind it.

It shows the argument can be written as rules and run, produces a layered organisation, and every decision can be traced. It does not yet show how much conditioned area the gradient saves against an all-controlled layout; the baseline and computational optimisation are next. Nor whether the five stages match real thermal protection, or whether tempered is comfortable in a Vancouver winter.

### 10. What comes next, and two questions (0:20)
[Screen] Scroll to What comes next. The roadmap still on Research; the Venn returns with the closing line.

The next step stays in research. The link between activity and space is the thinnest part: more case studies, and a kit-of-parts built from them, so each strategy can be placed on the gradient and tested in the tool.

Two questions. Is the gradient convincing without local measurements? And where does the logic from activity to space to decision need straightening out? Thank you.
